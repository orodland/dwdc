# Confine AI to extraction, keep matching deterministic

An AI-enabled reconciliation of year-end broker confirmations against a commodities trader's internal list of open trades is **technically feasible now**, with one condition: AI must not be the thing that decides. The leading 2026 pattern is a staged pipeline. Deterministic parsing reads every native PDF text layer and Excel cell. AI is confined to understanding layouts, onboarding new formats, providing a fallback for scans and sorting exceptions. A rules-based matching engine produces every match status, and the evidence of record is a frozen, hashed extraction artifact rather than a fresh model call. The evidence says the hard technical problem is **complete** extraction from long multi-page statements, not accuracy on individual cells. Benchmarks show systems tend to **drop rows rather than misread them** ([ExtractBench, arXiv 2607.29677](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.29677)), and hosted LLMs are **not repeatable even at temperature 0** ([Thinking Machines Lab](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/defeating-nondeterminism-in-llm-inference)). Matching hundreds of thousands of trades is computationally trivial. The binding constraints on this project are therefore mainly data-related: format variety, symbology and price-unit mapping, provenance of the broker documents, and quality of the internal list. Next come process and organisational constraints: firm methodology sign-off, tool certification under ISQM 1, auditor secrecy and cross-border data approvals, and reviewer workflow. Technology comes last. Regulators across the IAASB, PCAOB, FRC, IFIAR and the Swiss FAOA have issued no AI-specific auditing standard. Existing standards apply in full and human oversight is treated as non-negotiable, so a design that keeps matching deterministic and every AI contribution reviewable fits every framework examined. Several premises need correcting: ISA 500 (Revised) is **not** final, SR 11-7 was **rescinded in April 2026**, and EU AI Act high-risk dates moved. The prototype can be built and scored entirely on synthetic data before any client document is touched.

## Executive summary for the engagement and technology teams

**Recommendation.** Treat the solution as an auditor-controlled evidence pipeline with AI at the edges, not as an "AI reconciliation agent". Build it so that four things are true. Every broker trade row can be traced to a file hash and a page and bounding box, or a sheet and cell. Every normalised value records the deterministic transformations applied to it. Every match status comes from a named, versioned rule. Rerunning the reconciliation from the frozen inputs produces a byte-identical output. AI earns its place in four bounded jobs. It proposes column and header mappings and per-broker parser templates, which a human approves and freezes as configuration. It extracts from scanned or never-seen layouts as a second, independent path whose output is always verified. It proposes symbology mappings. It drafts classifications and explanations of breaks. It never fills a missing field, never accepts an uncertain match and never closes an exception.

**Why this design.** Three bodies of evidence point the same way. Frontier multimodal models are strong on table structure but imperfect at scale: about **0.89–0.91 GriTS** on full multi-page tables for leading 2026 models ([POTATR paper, arXiv 2606.09788](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.09788)). Reported single-prompt accuracy on complex derivatives trade text is about 80% ([Xceptor confirmations summary](https://www.xceptor.com/hubfs/Xceptor%20Confirmations%20Agents.pdf)). One benchmark found that **0.949 field-level accuracy translated into only 0.700 whole-document accuracy** ([arXiv 2603.18014](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.18014)). None of these levels is acceptable for audit evidence without verification layers. Hosted LLM inference is not bit-reproducible: in one experiment, 1,000 identical temperature-0 prompts gave about 80 distinct outputs ([Thinking Machines Lab](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/defeating-nondeterminism-in-llm-inference)). Regulators expect firm-level certification, engagement-level understanding, documented human challenge and re-performability ([FRC AI guidance, June 2025](https://www.frc.org.uk/news-and-events/news/2025/06/frc-publishes-landmark-guidance-providing-clarity-to-audit-profession-on-the-uses-of-ai/); [IAASB roundtable feedback, Feb 2026](https://www.iaasb.org/news-events/2026-02/iaasb-publishes-global-roundtable-feedback-technology-and-quality-management)).

**How to start.** Run a five-gate proof of concept on a **synthetic** trade register and **synthetic broker statements** rendered in eight to twelve layouts: native PDF, scanned PDF and Excel. Seed the broker side with known breaks. Because the generator knows the truth, it produces the ground-truth match file automatically. Set acceptance criteria before building. The hard criteria are: zero false matches; 100% row completeness reconciled to statement totals; 100% recall of seeded breaks; byte-identical reruns of the deterministic stages; zero undetected critical-field errors in auto-accepted rows on a sealed test set. Measured criteria are reviewer minutes against a timed manual baseline and the time to onboard a held-back, never-seen broker format. Only after these gates pass should the team seek approvals for a parallel run on a live engagement.

| Requested item | Short answer |
|---|---|
| (1) Technical feasibility | Feasible. Native PDF and Excel extraction can be near-lossless with deterministic parsers. Scans and unseen layouts need AI plus verification. Matching at 10^5–10^6 trades is a hash-join problem measured in seconds. |
| (2) Architecture | Nine stages: evidence intake → parse → format classification → extraction (template first, AI fallback) → validation and normalisation → tiered deterministic matching → exception classification → human review → audit trail and evidence pack, with QA and monitoring across all stages. |
| (3) AI vs deterministic | AI proposes (mappings, templates, scan extraction, symbology candidates, break explanations). Code decides (parsing of known formats, normalisation, every validation, every match, every status). |

Repeatability: freeze and hash, with templates in place of model calls. Auditability: per-trade lineage record and an append-only event log. |
| (5) Main risks | Silent row omission; plausible fabricated numbers; non-determinism and model retirement; symbology and unit mapping errors; automation bias in review; secrecy and data-transfer exposure; tool not certified or not used as intended. |
| (6) PoC | Five gates, synthetic data first, success criteria fixed at Gate 0, a sealed golden test set, a scale test of at least 200,000 trades, two held-back formats to time onboarding. |
| Framing question | Data and process/organisational constraints dominate. Technology is a solvable, secondary constraint. |

## Data and process, not models, are the binding constraints

The auditor asked whether the main constraints are technological, process-related, data-related or organisational. The evidence answers clearly: **data first, then process and organisation, with technology a distant fourth.** The technology pieces exist and are well characterised. The pieces that decide whether the tool produces audit evidence are about data and governance.

The data constraints are concrete. Broker statements come in semi-fixed layouts that repeat per broker. Most matching failures in derivatives reconciliation are representation differences rather than real economic breaks: broker versus exchange symbology, month codes, single-digit years, fractional price displays, cents-versus-dollars units, contract multipliers and sign conventions. Corn at 498.25 cents per bushel can be displayed as "498'2" ([Databento, fractional prices](https://databento.com/docs/examples/instrument-definitions/fractional-prices)). Grain futures quote in cents per bushel while the value per point differs from contract size ([FlashAlpha, CME multipliers](https://flashalpha.com/articles/cme-futures-contract-multipliers-quote-conventions)). FX value dates depend on currency-pair spot conventions and platform-specific holiday rules: spot is T+2 for most pairs and T+1 for USD/CAD ([FX date conventions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_exchange_date_conventions)), and the CME/EBS and OSTTRA calendars treat holidays differently ([CME EBS value date calendar](https://www.cmegroup.com/content/dam/cmegroup/documents/ebs_value-date-calendar.pdf); [OSTTRA SEF document](https://osttra.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2019-07-sef-unexpected-event-impact.pdf)). Give-ups, allocations and average-price (APS) trades break the one-to-one assumption. A broker can show one average-price line where the internal book holds the original street fills ([FIA average pricing best practice](https://fia.org/sites/default/files/2019-10/3%20%28i%29%20-%20Best%20Practice%20Recommendations%20for%20Average%20Pricing%20%28indirect%20clearing%29.pdf)). Standards such as CFI, FIGI, UPI and the ISDA CDM help but cover no broker statement end to end. The CDM itself is "NOT an application in and of itself" ([FINOS CDM overview](https://cdm.finos.org/docs/cdm-overview)). A maintained, versioned symbology and unit map is therefore unavoidable. So is a defined ground truth: counterparty documents contain errors, and the tool must report "the document says X, the client says Y" rather than correct either side ([Confirmation.com SKM case study](https://www.confirmation.com/resources/case-studies/schaffner,-knight,-minnaugh--co.aspx)).

The process and organisational constraints are equally concrete. Gartner predicted that at least **30% of GenAI projects would be abandoned after proof of concept by end-2025**, citing poor data quality, inadequate risk controls, escalating costs or unclear business value ([Gartner, July 2024](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2024-07-29-gartner-predicts-30-percent-of-generative-ai-projects-will-be-abandoned-after-proof-of-concept-by-end-of-2025)). The widely quoted MIT NANDA finding that about 95% of pilots stall attributes the gap to brittle workflows and poor operational fit rather than model quality. Its methodology is described inconsistently and it is a preliminary research programme, so treat the number as directional ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/08/18/mit-report-95-percent-generative-ai-pilots-at-companies-failing-cfo/); [Let's Data Science analysis](https://letsdatascience.com/news/mit-report-documents-genai-pilot-roi-gap-e0924d7d)). The audit-specific evidence is sharper still. In IFIAR's 2024 inspection survey, **31% of member regulators reported findings where audit technology tools were not used as intended**, up from 29% the year before ([IFIAR 2025 technology report](https://www.ifiar.org/latest-news/ifiar-releases-2025-report-on-the-use-of-technology-in-audits/)). The FRC's 2025 thematic review found that the six largest UK firms had mature tool-certification processes, but press reports say all but one had no KPIs for how those tools affect audit quality ([FRC thematic review](https://mail.caalley.com/intl_cnt/frc/FRC_Thematic_Review_on_the_Certification_of_Automated_Tools_and_Techniques.pdf); [Scottish Financial News](https://www.scottishfinancialnews.com/articles/frc-major-accounting-firms-using-ai-without-quality-oversight)). For a Swiss auditor there is a further gating item: deciding whether, and how, secrecy-protected client data may reach any third-party model under Art. 321 StGB. That decision is legal and organisational, not technical.

Technology still matters at three specific points: complete extraction from long multi-page tables, scanned documents, and non-deterministic inference. Each has a known architectural answer, set out below. The case for "technology is the constraint" is strongest for scanned, phone-captured or fax-quality statements. There, financial-document benchmarks show markedly lower scores than clean-page benchmarks ([FinixDoc, arXiv 2608.22842](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.22842)). A PoC should measure how much of the real broker population is in that state before concluding either way.

## 1. Technical feasibility: matching is solved, complete extraction is the hard part

**Native PDF and Excel extraction is feasible to near-lossless standards; the gap is completeness on long tables and robustness on scans.** For Excel, deterministic libraries read the stored cell values and formats exactly, with no OCR risk. The only job that needs intelligence is understanding structure: where the table starts, which row is the header, how merged headers map to the canonical schema. Even purpose-built LLM spreadsheet encodings get table-region detection only about **78.9% F1** right ([SpreadsheetLLM, Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/encoding-spreadsheets-for-large-language-models/)). That is why an AI-proposed mapping must be approved and frozen as configuration rather than inferred afresh on each run. For native PDFs, layout-aware parsers combined with the text layer perform strongly. One hybrid study found a table-based parser reached near-perfect F1 on PDFs at about 1.6 seconds per document, while direct LLM extraction was **over 40 times slower** ([arXiv 2510.10138](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.10138v1)). Benchmark results conflict on which open-source parser leads. The PDFBench write-up puts pdfplumber at 93.4% table TEDS ([LiveInTheFuture benchmark](https://liveinthefuture.org/stories/pdf-to-markdown-conversion-token-formatting-benchmark.html)), while a 2025 scientific-PDF benchmark ranks Docling strongest ([arXiv 2511.16134](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.16134)). Parser choice should therefore be settled empirically on the target broker layouts.

**Frontier models are good at structure and not good enough on their own.** On Kensho's PubTables-v2 full documents with multi-page tables, the POTATR paper reports Claude Opus 4.6 at GriTS_Con 0.9079 and GPT-5.4 at 0.8847 ([arXiv 2606.09788](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.09788)). A search summary reports Gemini 3.1 Pro leading page-level results at about 0.942 ([PubTables-v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10888v3)). These model names are illustrative examples, not recommendations. Small specialised table models are competitive or better on single pages at a fraction of the cost: POTATR reports about 130 times faster and 300 times cheaper than frontier models ([POTATR summary](https://chatpaper.com/paper/297232)). Frontier models keep an edge in stitching tables across pages. The two dominant failure modes matter more than headline accuracy. The first is **dropped or truncated records** on long documents, which ExtractBench attributes to context and output limits in systems without an iteration strategy ([arXiv 2607.29677](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.29677)). The second is **plausible but hallucinated numbers**. A vendor-run benchmark reports 72.6–85.0% hallucination-free accuracy for general VLMs against 93.2% for a specialised OCR model, and flags financial documents as a sensitive case ([PP-OCRv6, arXiv 2606.13108](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.13108)). On derivatives text specifically, CompatibL's 2024 hackathon found well-crafted single prompts reached about 90–95% on simple trades and about 80% on complex ones. It also found models "doing too much", applying transformations not valid for the trade ([as summarised in Xceptor's confirmations paper](https://www.xceptor.com/hubfs/Xceptor%20Confirmations%20Agents.pdf)). No published benchmark exists for derivatives broker statements or multi-page trade blotters. Every accuracy figure here is borrowed from adjacent document types.

**The architecture closes the gap rather than waiting for better models.** The "Compiled AI" pattern fixes prompt, schema and triggers at compile time and calls the LLM only as a bounded tool. On invoice extraction it matched direct-LLM key-field accuracy (80.0%), gave the best line-item accuracy (80.4%) and ran about 2.3 times faster ([arXiv 2604.05150](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05150v1)). Microsoft's engineering team separated deterministic extraction from LLM inference after single-prompt designs gave inconsistent output. When the two disagree, they take the deterministic source value ([Microsoft ISE](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/ise/separating-deterministic-extraction-from-ai-inference/)). Even a vendor of LLM parsing says fixed-format, high-volume documents are often better served by traditional parsing ([LlamaIndex glossary](https://www.llamaindex.ai/glossary/prompt-based-document-parsing)). Broker statements are exactly that kind of document.

**Matching and scale are not a feasibility issue.** Exact-key matching at 10^5–10^7 records is a hash-join problem. In a single-author test, DuckDB joined 300 million rows against a 5-million-row dimension in 1.72 seconds using 0.70 GB on a laptop ([Python in Plain English](https://python.plainenglish.io/i-gave-a-16-gb-mac-a-26-gb-dataset-duckdb-answered-in-440-mb-of-ram-58db5f5acd4b)). Benchmarks conflict on Polars versus DuckDB, so treat engine choice as a local test ([Uvik](https://uvik.net/blog/polars-vs-pandas/)). Extraction cost is also modest when deterministic paths carry the load. Managed parsers list at roughly $0.004–$0.045 per page ([CodeSOTA](https://codesota.com/ocr/reducto-vs-open-source); [Moda](https://moda.app/blog/docling-alternatives)). As an illustration on our own assumption of 30–50 trade rows per page, 300,000 trades would be about 6,000–10,000 pages, or a few hundred dollars per cycle even at the top managed-parser rate. Self-hosted template parsing costs almost nothing per page. The cost that scales is human review, which is why routing precision matters more than compute.

## 2. Proposed end-to-end architecture: nine stages around a frozen evidence record

The architecture is a fixed, staged workflow, not an open-ended agent. This follows Anthropic's own guidance: use predefined code paths for well-defined tasks and add agentic complexity only when needed ([Anthropic, Building effective agents](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-effective-agents)). It is also consistent with Gartner's forecast that over **40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by end-2027** for cost, value or risk-control reasons ([New Electronics on Gartner](https://www.newelectronics.co.uk/content/news/over-40-of-agentic-ai-projects-could-be-cancelled-by-2027)). Cloud reference architectures converge on the same shape: ingestion, OCR/parse, classification, extraction, validation, human-in-the-loop review and export ([AWS GenAI IDP Accelerator](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/accelerate-intelligent-document-processing-with-generative-ai-on-aws)). The design below adds what those generic architectures lack: audit-grade lineage and a deterministic matching core.

```
[A] Evidence intake & register ──► [B] Parse (text layer / cells / OCR) ──► [C] Format fingerprint & routing
        │ file hash, sender,              │ page+bbox / sheet!cell              │ known template │ unknown → onboarding queue
        ▼                                 ▼                                     ▼
[D] Extraction: Path 1 deterministic template parser  +  Path 2 independent AI/VLM extraction (scans, new formats, verification)
        ▼  raw strings + provenance, never imputed
[E] Validation & normalisation (pure functions, versioned reference data) ──► completeness & control-total gates
        ▼  canonical trade records
[F] Deterministic matching waterfall T0…T4 (internal list ⇄ broker records) ──► statuses from named rules only
        ▼
[G] Exception classification ──► [H] Human review (source-first UI, reason codes) ──► dispositions as new events
        ▼
[I] Audit trail: append-only event log, per-trade lineage, reproducibility package, working-paper export
        ║
[Q] QA & monitoring across all stages: golden-set regression, sampling, drift, reviewer vigilance, model-change control
```

### Document ingestion and data extraction are two separate stages

**Ingestion (stage A)** is an evidence-control step, not a technical convenience. Each broker document enters an evidence register. The register records its SHA-256 hash; its channel and sender metadata (direct from broker, broker portal accessed by the auditor, or supplied by the client); the receipt timestamp; the broker identifier; and its role (year-end open-position statement, individual confirmation, or both). Originals are immutable. Entity personnel cannot upload or edit responses. Unexpected channels or senders are flagged as possible fraud indicators rather than data-quality noise. This is the mechanism by which the auditor "maintains control" in the sense of ISA 505 and AS 2310 (section on regulatory perspectives below). It also determines whether the documents are external confirmations or company-provided external information, which have different evidential consequences.

**Parsing and format classification (stages B–C)** turn each file into text and tables with coordinates. For a native PDF this means the text layer with page and bounding box. For Excel it means openpyxl-class inspection that captures sheets, merged ranges, number formats, hidden rows and sheets, and formulas versus cached values. Scans get OCR. Every document is then fingerprinted by layout or template hash, header tokens, column set and sender. Known fingerprints route to an approved template. Unknown fingerprints route to an onboarding queue, never to auto-accept.

**Extraction (stage D)** runs two genuinely independent paths. Path 1 is a deterministic, human-approved, version-controlled template or parser for each broker format. An AI may have drafted the parser, but it runs as code. Path 2 is an AI or vision-model extraction. It is the primary path only for scans and formats still in onboarding, and elsewhere it serves as a verification pass or a periodic sample check. Extraction emits **raw strings exactly as printed** (for example `price_raw: "1.234,50"`) together with location provenance. It does not normalise, infer or fill gaps. A flat per-row schema is preferred to a deep one, because constrained decoding guarantees valid structure but not correct values. On complex schemas, structured-output mode has been shown to *lower* accuracy: validity fell from 51% to 37% in one benchmark ([ExtractBench, arXiv 2602.12247](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.12247)). A preprint reports schema validity rising from 61.5% to 100% while answer accuracy fell from 19.7% to 11.0% ([Constraint Tax preprint](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.26128.md)).

### Data validation and normalisation is pure, versioned code

**Validation (stage E, first half)** has three layers. Syntactic checks cover schema validity, required fields and types. Semantic checks cover ISO 4217 currencies, MIC codes, valid dates, trade date no later than value or settlement date, strike present for options, and price × quantity × multiplier ≈ notional where the statement prints a notional. Population checks compare the extracted row count per page and section to the rows detected by a deterministic line or table detector and to any printed trade count, compare sums of quantities and amounts to statement footers and summary sheets, check page and sequence continuity, and check that trade IDs are unique across page boundaries. Any population failure fails the document as a whole. It is not a row-level warning.

**Normalisation (stage E, second half)** converts raw strings into canonical values with pure functions. Fractional and locale-formatted prices become decimals. Cents become currency units through a per-product price-unit table. B/S, +/− and long/short collapse to a signed-quantity convention. Broker codes such as "ZCZ6", "C Z26" or "CN Dec26" resolve through a versioned symbology map. Single-digit years are disambiguated with a documented decade rule anchored to the trade date. FX deals are expressed as bought currency and amount versus sold currency and amount. Every transformation has an ID and version, and both the raw and normalised values are kept. Ambiguity is never resolved by guessing. A date that could be DD/MM or MM/DD, absent a broker-template rule, becomes an extraction-uncertainty exception. The canonical instrument key follows the industry pattern: `{MIC}|{product_root}|{YYYYMM}` for futures, plus put/call, strike in canonical units and expiry for options on futures, and `{ccy_bought}|{ccy_sold}|{value_date}` for FX forwards.

The canonical record is identical regardless of broker or format. That is what makes outputs standardised.

| Field group | Canonical fields (illustrative) | Provenance carried per field |
|---|---|---|
| Identity | broker_id, account, broker_trade_ref, exchange_exec_id (if any), client_ref | source_doc_sha256, page + bbox or sheet!cell, raw string |
| Instrument | product_type (FUT/OOF/FXFWD), instrument_key, MIC, product_root, contract_month, put_call, strike, expiry | raw code, symbology-map version |
| Economics | side, signed_qty, price (canonical units), currency, multiplier; FX: bought ccy/amt, sold ccy/amt, rate | raw strings, transformation IDs |
| Dates | trade_date, value/settle_date, expiry | raw strings, date-rule ID |
| Grouping | avg_px_group_id, allocation_group_id, order_ref | raw strings |
| Control | extraction_path (template/AI), template or model version, grounding result, dual-path agreement flag, validation results | run ID |

### Deterministic trade matching follows a strict waterfall

**Matching (stage F)** consumes canonical records only. It runs as a waterfall from the strictest rule to the loosest, as enterprise engines and the reconciliation patent literature describe ([USPTO 8103561](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8103561); [Oracle match rules](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/account-reconcile-cloud/suarc/admin_trans_match_about_match_rules.html)). Each tier sees only the records not consumed by earlier tiers. T0 matches on a shared unique reference (broker trade ID, exchange execution ID, or the internal ID echoed in the broker's client-reference field) plus full economic equality. T1 matches on the full composite key (account, instrument key, side, trade date) with exact quantity and price. T2 allows a price difference only up to an auditor-set, documented rounding allowance, at most one tick, and labels the result `MATCHED_WITH_TOLERANCE` so it is never confused with an exact match. T3 handles one-to-many and many-to-one links (APS groups, partial fills, splits) only where an explicit group key exists or the block is small and capped. It requires exact Σqty and checks VWAP within a bound, with the APS cash residual reported as a separate component rather than absorbed into a tolerance ([OnixS CME STP PositionAmountType](https://ref.onixs.biz/net-cme-stp-handler-guide/p-onixs-cmestphandler-positionamountdata-positionamounttype.html)). T4 is a **position-level** check of net signed quantity per account and instrument key at zero tolerance. It explains aggregation residue but never converts a trade-level exception into a match. For FX forwards, the compared fields follow confirmation-matching practice: counterparty, trade date, value date, bought and sold currency and amount, and rate ([Oracle Treasury MT300 rules](https://docs.oracle.com/en/industries/financial-services/banking-treasury-management/14.7.3.0.0/trfxm/confirmation-rule-maintenance-details.html)). Any allowance applies only to the derived contra amount.

**Ambiguity is never resolved probabilistically.** If a record has more than one qualifying candidate within a tier, the engine marks it `AMBIGUOUS`. The one exception is when the candidates are economically interchangeable, for example two identical one-lot fills. Then pairing is by stable rank: sort both sides by key, trade time and immutable source row ID, and zip by row number. Probabilistic record-linkage scoring of the Fellegi-Sunter kind, with a clerical-review band between thresholds ([UK Government Splink blog](https://dataingovernment.blog.gov.uk/2022/09/23/splink-fast-accurate-and-scalable-record-linkage/)), may at most *suggest* candidates for human review in a separate layer. It never enters the confirmed match set, mirroring Oracle's "suggested versus confirmed" distinction. Determinism comes from fixed rule order, pure normalisation functions, explicit `ORDER BY` with a unique tie-breaker before any "first match wins" logic (parallel engines do not guarantee row order), one-to-one consumption of matched IDs, and snapshots of rules and reference data hashed into every run.

### Exception management separates "could not compare" from "disagrees"

**Exception classification (stage G)** gives each unmatched or broken item exactly one primary code. Each code carries the rule tier reached, the candidate IDs considered and field-level differences. The most important design choice is to keep failures of *representation or extraction* apart from *economic* breaks. Then an auditor can tell "the data could not be compared" from "the data disagrees". The requirement to identify unmatched items, missing fields, duplicates, extraction uncertainties and conflicting values separately maps onto the taxonomy below. The core of it is drawn from industry break categories, extended for document extraction.

| Category | Codes | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Unmatched | UNMATCHED_INTERNAL, UNMATCHED_BROKER | Open trade on one side with no counterpart; a ±1 business day same-economics search adds a "probable timing" label without matching |
| Economic break | QTY_BREAK, PRICE_BREAK, SIDE_BREAK, DATE_BREAK, GROUP_SUM_BREAK, POSITION_BREAK | Key agrees, economics disagree |
| Static data | INSTRUMENT_MISMATCH, ACCOUNT_MISMATCH | Symbology or account alias missing or conflicting |
| Missing fields | MISSING_FIELD | Required value absent in source; never imputed |
| Duplicates | DUPLICATE_BROKER, DUPLICATE_INTERNAL | Same reference and economics twice in one source, including across page breaks |
| Extraction uncertainty | EXTRACTION_UNCERTAIN, PARSE_ERROR, COMPLETENESS_FAIL | Dual-path disagreement, ungrounded value, ambiguous date or number format, row-count or control-total failure |
| Conflicting values | CONFLICTING_SOURCE_VALUES | Two broker documents (e.g., confirmation vs year-end statement, PDF vs Excel version) disagree on the same trade |
| Ambiguity | AMBIGUOUS | More than one equally valid candidate |
| Clearing workflow | ALLOC_INCOMPLETE | Give-up awaiting allocation (e.g., CME AllocInd=1) ([CME STP FIXML](https://cmegroupclientsite.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EPICSANDBOX/pages/599064941)) |

### Human review decides dispositions but never rewrites machine results

**Human review (stage H)** is where audit judgement enters. Its design has to counter automation bias, which is documented in auditors: in an experiment with 119 professional auditors, participants substituted automated cues for careful information-seeking ([Foundation for Auditing Research](https://foundationforauditingresearch.org/publications/auditor-automation-usage-and-professional-skepticism/)). A 2025 review of 35 studies found that explanation features often fail to reduce automation bias, while designs that force active verification work better ([AI & Society review, via listing](https://www.longtermwiki.com/resources/a96cbf6f98644f2f)). The reviewer screen therefore puts the source page image, with each field's bounding box highlighted, next to the extracted values. It asks for explicit per-field confirmation of critical fields: quantity, price or strike, side, instrument, dates and account. It reveals the internal counterpart record **only after** the extracted values are confirmed, so the reviewer cannot anchor on the client's book. It requires a reason code for every override and disables bulk approval for high-risk strata. A reviewer's decision is stored as a new event, a *disposition*, layered over the machine status, which stays unchanged. That preserves the separation between what the rules found and what the auditor concluded, which ISA 505 and AS 2310 expect for exception investigation.

### Audit-trail generation is a by-product of the event log

**The audit trail (stage I)** is not a report written at the end. It is an append-only, event-sourced log in which every step is an immutable event, for example DocumentReceived, ExtractionProduced, ValidationFailed, ReconRun, ReviewDisposition. Events are hash-chained or anchored by periodic Merkle roots for tamper evidence and stored with a retention lock. Open lineage vocabularies are available to export it: W3C PROV for entity, activity and agent semantics ([W3C PROV-Overview](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-overview/)) and OpenLineage for pipeline run events ([OpenLineage spec](https://openlineage.io/docs/spec/object-model)). The per-trade record answers the full audit-trail requirement.

| Per-trade audit-trail element | Content |
|---|---|
| Source document and location | source_doc_sha256, evidence-register entry (channel, sender, timestamp), page + bounding box or sheet!cell, verbatim snippet |
| Extracted values | raw strings per field, extraction path (template/AI/human), template commit SHA or model snapshot ID, prompt hash and parameters, grounding result |
| Transformations | ordered list of normalisation function IDs and versions, reference-data snapshot hashes, raw → canonical values |
| Validation | each check with pass/fail, completeness and control-total results for the parent document |
| Matching rule | tier and rule ID, rule-set version, tolerance applied (if any) |
| Counterpart record | internal trade-list row ID and file hash, or "none" |
| Final status | machine status and code, reviewer disposition, reviewer ID, timestamp, reason, before/after values |
| Run | run ID, engine commit SHA, input and output dataset hashes |

For each cycle, the system exports a **reproducibility package**: frozen inputs, frozen extraction outputs, configuration, prompt and parser versions, golden-set results for the deployed version, control-test evidence, and a script that re-runs the deterministic reconciliation to a byte-identical output hash. An inspector can re-perform the reconciliation without calling any model.

### Quality assurance and monitoring sit outside the run path

**QA and monitoring (stage Q)** treat the AI extractor as a component under validation. Any change to a model snapshot, prompt, schema, parser, OCR engine or decoding parameters is a change event. It re-runs the full golden set and must meet or beat the incumbent in every stratum, with a stored old-versus-new diff and a sign-off. In production, a random sample of auto-accepted rows is re-performed by humans, alongside targeted samples of low-confidence and novel-format items. Monitoring tracks, per broker, validation-failure rates, override rates, null rates and breaks attributable to extraction. Reviewer vigilance is measured by injecting seeded errors into review queues and tracking catch rates and dwell times. Near-100% approval with very short dwell times is the signature of rubber-stamping. Snapshot retirement dates for every model in use are logged against provider schedules. For example, Anthropic retired Claude Opus 3 on 5 January 2026 under its published process ([Anthropic deprecation update](https://www.anthropic.com/research/deprecation-updates-opus-3)), and a migration needs re-validation, not assumption ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/axrisi/api-deprecation-for-ai-models-what-breaks-when-a-model-is-retired-1a01)).

## 3. AI versus deterministic processing: AI proposes, code decides

The dividing rule is simple. **Anything that produces or changes an audit-relevant value or status is deterministic. Anything AI produces is a proposal that is either verified by code or approved by a human, and then frozen.** This matches credible industry practice, where AI sits around the matching core (rule suggestion, break classification, exception routing) while the confirmed match set comes from deterministic rules ([Hedgeweek on Electra](https://www.hedgeweek.com/electra-adds-new-capabilities-reconciliation-platform/); [FIS cleared derivatives](https://www.fisglobal.com/-/media/fisglobal/files/pdf/product-sheet/cleared-derivatives-reconciliations-product-sheet.pdf)). It also matches the regulators' view that AI does not shift responsibility.

| Component | Processing | Role of AI | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Evidence intake, hashing, channel checks | Deterministic | None | Evidence control must be exact |
| PDF text-layer and Excel cell reading | Deterministic | None | Lossless; no OCR or hallucination risk |
| OCR of scans | Specialised model (non-generative OCR) | Optional VLM second read | Dual reading catches digit and homoglyph confusion |
| Format fingerprinting | Deterministic | AI may label an unknown format for onboarding | Unknown must route to onboarding, never auto-accept |
| Template/parser for a known broker format | Deterministic at run time | AI drafts parser or column mapping once; human approves; frozen in version control | Determinism comes from code, not the model |
| Extraction from scans and unseen layouts | AI (VLM/LLM), then verified | Primary path only until a template exists | Highest error risk; always grounded and dual-checked |
| Validation and control totals | Deterministic | None | These are the controls over AI output |
| Normalisation, symbology, units | Deterministic, versioned tables | AI may propose new map entries; human approves | Mapping errors are silent and systematic |
| Matching waterfall and statuses | Deterministic | None | No probabilistic acceptance |
| Candidate suggestions for AMBIGUOUS/UNMATCHED | Outside match set | AI or probabilistic scoring may suggest, with explicit scores | Suggestion only; human records the decision |
| Exception classification | Deterministic primary code | AI drafts explanation or probable root cause | Primary code must be reproducible |
| Review dispositions | Human | AI may pre-draft notes, labelled as such | Judgement stays with the auditor |
| Evidence pack and audit trail | Deterministic | None | Must re-perform byte-identically |
| QA triage | Deterministic metrics | LLM-as-judge only as a prioritiser, validated against human labels | Judges have position, verbosity and self-preference biases ([Zheng et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.05685); [arXiv 2410.21819](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21819v1)) |

Model risk teams should note a practical consequence. Under the "LLM authors parsers" pattern, the run-time artifact for known formats is ordinary code under software change control, and the AI component reduces to a data-capture aid with compensating controls. That shrinks the model-risk surface and simplifies firm certification. The framing should be agreed with methodology and quality teams at the outset rather than argued afterwards.

## 4. Controlling accuracy, completeness, repeatability and auditability

### Accuracy rests on grounding, independent dual extraction and business rules

The strongest single control against fabricated values is a **deterministic grounding check**. Every extracted value must carry a source span, and code confirms that the value occurs verbatim in that region of the text layer or cell. With PyMuPDF, for example, the model returns the literal value and `search_for` locates it, so the PDF's text positions are the source of record ([Artifex on grounding](https://artifex.com/blog/grounding-in-document-extraction)). A value with no locatable span is rejected and becomes EXTRACTION_UNCERTAIN. Scans have no text layer, so the substitute is two independent reads (a conventional OCR pass and a VLM) compared field by field, plus arithmetic checks. Line items must sum to statement totals, because VLM errors look plausible and slip past spell-checks ([Edge of Context OCR guide](https://slavadubrov.github.io/blog/2026/03/04/ocr-guide/)). Independence of method matters more than more samples. Resampling the same model reaches only 0.744 ROC AUC as an error detector at about five times the cost, and mean log-probability gives about 0.705 ([Beyond Logprobs, arXiv 2606.24420](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.24420)). Single-model confidence is therefore not a routing signal on its own. Thresholds should be calibrated per broker format on labelled data, and all of the following route to review: any disagreement, missing grounding, or business-rule failure. One subtle risk deserves explicit design. An extraction error that happens to agree with a wrong internal record will not show up as a break. **Extraction controls must stand on their own and must not rely on the reconciliation to catch errors.**

### Completeness is controlled at page, document and population level

Because the dominant long-document failure is silent omission, completeness controls carry as much weight as accuracy controls. At page level, the extracted row count must equal the rows found by a deterministic row detector, and repeated headers across page breaks are detected and removed rather than turned into rows. At document level, row counts and quantity, notional and amount totals must agree with printed trade counts, footers or summary sheets. Where the statement reports positions, opening position plus trades must equal closing position. For Excel, hidden rows and filtered views are surfaced, subtotal rows are excluded by rule, and formula cells are compared with their cached values. At population level, the broker population is reconciled to the evidence register: every expected broker and account has a document, and every document has a terminal state. The internal trade list is reconciled to the entity's derivative subledger by count and notional before matching starts. Under PCAOB AS 1105 .10A and extant ISA 500 para 9, the auditor must address the reliability of that list as information produced by the entity. If AI must extract long tables, it works page by page or table by table with explicit per-chunk row counts and continuation markers, never whole-document.

### Repeatability comes from freezing outputs and moving determinism into code

Temperature 0 does not give repeatable hosted output. The main cause is that inference kernels are not batch-invariant, so server load changes the numerics of a single request ([Thinking Machines Lab](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/defeating-nondeterminism-in-llm-inference)). OpenAI documents its `seed` parameter as best effort only, with a `system_fingerprint` to detect backend changes ([OpenAI advanced usage](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/advanced-usage)). Self-hosted stacks can be made deterministic with batch-invariant modes in vLLM or SGLang, at a reported throughput cost. One source gives about 34% for SGLang's deterministic mode, while secondary summaries of the original post cite about 61.5%. These are secondhand figures ([Spheron](https://www.spheron.network/blog/vllm-batch-invariance-why-temperature-0-isnt-deterministic/)). The defensible audit pattern therefore has four elements. First, hash every source file. Second, extract once and persist the output, provenance, model snapshot ID, prompt or template hash and validation results as an immutable artifact. Third, have the reconciliation read only frozen artifacts. Fourth, treat any re-extraction as a new, versioned run with a diff report. Where formats recur, AI-authored but human-approved templates replace model calls altogether. Run-to-run disagreement on the AI path is useful in its own right: running the AI fallback two or three times and flagging any field-level disagreement turns non-determinism into a cheap uncertainty detector. This is our design inference; we found no published guidance on it.

### Auditability means an inspector can trace and re-perform without the model

The regulators' common denominator is that an inspector can understand what the tool did, with what data, and how its outputs were evaluated, including false positives and false negatives. CalCPA reports, from secondary sources, inspection findings on AI anomaly tools that lacked documentation of calibration, input data and false-positive evaluation, treated as deficiencies regardless of whether the output was correct ([CalCPA](https://www.calcpa.org/whats-happening/articles/ai-standard-setting-regulation-in-audit-part-1)). The per-trade lineage record, the event log, the golden-set results for the deployed version and the reproducibility package together meet that bar. Configuration lives in version control with pull-request review, which also gives segregation of duties. Zero-data-retention contracts with a model provider make the firm's own record the *only* record, so the firm's store must hold the inputs, prompts, raw responses and hashes.

## 5. Principal technical, data, governance and implementation risks

| # | Risk | Type | Likelihood without controls | Primary mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Silent row omission on long or multi-page statements | Technical | High | Page and document row counts, control totals, chunked extraction, COMPLETENESS_FAIL fails the document |
| R2 | Plausible fabricated or mis-read numbers (homoglyphs, digit swaps, sign flips) | Technical | Medium–high on scans | Verbatim grounding, dual independent extraction, arithmetic checks, never impute |
| R3 | Non-determinism and silent provider-side changes | Technical | Certain on hosted APIs | Freeze-and-hash, templates for known formats, pinned snapshots, canary and regression suite |
| R4 | Model retirement mid-cycle or between years | Technical / vendor | Medium | Retirement register, migration regression test, re-validation sign-off; self-hosted option |
| R5 | Symbology, unit, multiplier and date-convention mapping errors | Data | High | Versioned reference tables, raw values retained, INSTRUMENT_MISMATCH as a distinct code, human approval of map changes |
| R6 | APS, give-up and partial-fill structures that break one-to-one matching | Data | High for futures | Group keys, exact Σqty, capped many-to-one blocks, ALLOC_INCOMPLETE and GROUP_SUM_BREAK codes |
| R7 | Unreliable internal trade list (incomplete or altered) | Data | Medium | Reconcile to subledger; test under AS 1105 .10A / ISA 500 para 9 |
| R8 | Broker documents not obtained under auditor control, or broker itself wrong | Data / evidence | Medium | Evidence register with channel provenance; ground truth = what the document says |
| R9 | Correlated error: wrong extraction matches a wrong internal record | Technical / data | Low but severe | Extraction controls independent of matching; seeded-break tests include "matching errors" |
| R10 | Automation bias and rubber-stamping in review | Governance | High | Source-first UI, counterpart hidden until confirmation, per-field confirmation, seeded canaries, dwell-time monitoring |
| R11 | Tool not certified, or used outside its intended use | Governance | Medium | ISQM 1 certification, intended-use statement, engagement-level documentation, KPIs the FRC found missing |
| R12 | Confidentiality, secrecy and cross-border transfer breach | Governance / legal | Medium | Self-hosted or Swiss/EU-hosted models, customer-held keys, pseudonymisation, client consent, DPA terms |
| R13 | Tool or vendor in the confirmation transmission path becomes an "intermediary" | Governance | Low–medium | Run inside auditor environment; if not, obtain SOC/ISAE assurance and assess override risk |
| R14 | Independence threats if the tool or its output is handed to the client | Governance | Low | Tool and outputs stay with the auditor; independence function sign-off |
| R15 | PoC success criteria vague, or workflow not integrated into working papers | Implementation | High | Criteria fixed at Gate 0, evidence pack in firm format, methodology involved from day one |
| R16 | Vendor accuracy and STP claims taken as targets | Implementation | High | Measure on own golden set; treat claims as hypotheses |

Several of these deserve emphasis because they are easy to underrate. **R16** is pervasive. Published case studies are almost all vendor marketing with no baselines: "90%+ STP" for multi-asset confirmations ([Xceptor](https://www.xceptor.com/solutions/confirmations)), "98.5%+" match rates ([NeoXam](https://www.neoxam.com/aro/otc-derivatives-reconciliation-software/)), and over 99% auto-match on structured cleared-derivatives data, which is not a document AI result ([FIS](https://www.fisglobal.com/-/media/fisglobal/files/pdf/product-sheet/cleared-derivatives-reconciliations-product-sheet.pdf)). Even vendors concede that headline automation rates of around 70% reflect the easy matches, and that the remaining 30% consumes most staff time ([insightsoftware](https://insightsoftware.com/resources/the-reconciliation-lie-why-most-ai-reconciliation-still-dumps-work-on-your-team/)). An operations STP rate also measures something different from audit tolerance. In operations a break simply goes to an analyst. In audit, **one false match is an evidence failure**. **R5 and R6** are where most real-world breaks originate, and AI does not remove them. AI can only propose mapping entries faster. **R10** cannot be fixed by training alone. The interface design is the control.

## 6. Proof of concept: five gates, synthetic data first

No public auditor PoC plan or acceptance standard exists for AI reconciliation of confirmations. The design below is synthesised from IDP evaluation practice, audit sampling logic and the failure evidence above. It is deliberately vendor-neutral. Any component can be swapped for an equivalent, and named products are examples only.

### Scope and sample design

Scope the PoC to the year-end use case: open positions in listed commodity futures, options on futures and FX forwards, matched trade by trade to an internal open-trade list, with a position-level check. Valuation comparison is out of scope. Broker marks are evidence to be evaluated under ISA 540 / AS 2501 and should be kept separate from terms reconciliation. Stratify synthetic broker formats by difficulty. The **easy** tier covers native PDF with a clean single table, and CSV or XLSX. The **medium** tier covers multi-page tables with repeated headers, merged and multi-row headers, multiple accounts per statement, subtotal rows, and Excel with hidden rows and number formats that hide decimals. The **hard** tier covers scanned, rotated and fax-noised PDFs, locale variants (1.234,56 versus 1,234.56, DD/MM versus MM/DD), fractional price displays, and statements where APS lines stand in for fills. Eight to twelve formats are enough to exercise every path. Hold back **two** formats completely, one native PDF and one Excel, to measure onboarding time at Gate 2.

Use three dataset tiers. A **development set** of roughly 2,000–5,000 trades is used for building templates and prompts. A **sealed golden test set** of roughly 5,000–10,000 trades across all formats is never used for tuning. A **scale set** of at least 200,000–500,000 trades across brokers and two simulated year-end cycles tests throughput, repeatability and false-match rates at volume. The scale set matters for statistics. Under the "rule of three", zero errors in n independent trials supports an upper 95% confidence bound of about 3/n on the error rate ([Rule of three](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rule_of_three_(statistics))). So zero false matches across 200,000 matchable trades supports a false-match rate below about 0.0015%. Zero critical-field errors across about 300 sampled rows per stratum supports a rate below 1%, and 59 documents with no error support below 5%.

### Synthetic data generator

Build a Python generator (for example Faker plus domain rules) that first creates a **synthetic internal open-trade register**. It should use realistic product roots, contract months, strikes, multipliers, accounts, currency pairs and value dates, including holiday-adjusted FX dates and single-digit-year codes. The generator then derives each **broker's view** and renders it with HTML-to-PDF or reportlab and openpyxl in each broker layout. It produces scanned variants by rasterising and adding noise, rotation and compression. It injects **controlled, labelled perturbations** into the broker side, in two families. *Economic breaks* include missing trades, extra broker trades, quantity, price, side and date differences, duplicates within and across page breaks, trades closed internally but still open at the broker, incomplete allocations, and APS groups whose VWAP or Σqty does not reconcile. *Extraction stressors* include homoglyphs (O/0, l/1, 5/S), parenthesised negatives, rows split across pages, totals that deliberately disagree with rows, missing fields, ambiguous dates, and the same trade appearing in a confirmation and a statement with conflicting values. Because the generator knows the truth, it emits the ground-truth extraction JSON and the expected match outcome for every internal and broker trade automatically. That allows end-to-end scoring, including false-match rate, before any confidential data exists in the environment. It also gives the firm a reusable regression asset that does not depend on client data. Later, if approvals permit, layout-only replicas of real broker templates can be added, with synthetic values poured into real layouts.

### Golden dataset rules

The golden set is versioned, and its hash is recorded in every evaluation report. It is labelled at field level with source-span annotations. Synthetic data is labelled by the generator. Any real or semi-real documents are labelled independently by two people, with adjudication. Labels record three things: what the document says (not what is "true"), the expected match outcome per trade with the breaking field named, and whether the document itself is wrong. The test partition is sealed. Metrics are reported **per stratum and per broker**, never only in aggregate, because aggregates hide weak formats.

### Suggested component stack

| Layer | Vendor-neutral suggestion (illustrative examples) |
|---|---|
| Evidence register and storage | Object storage with retention lock or WORM option; SHA-256 manifests |
| Parse – native PDF | Text-layer and table tools such as PyMuPDF, pdfplumber, Docling (choose empirically per layout) |
| Parse – Excel/CSV | openpyxl-class inspection (merged ranges, formats, hidden rows, formulas vs cached values) + pandas/Polars |
| OCR – scans | A conventional OCR engine (open-source or managed) as the first read |
| AI extraction / onboarding | A frontier multimodal LLM via API for drafting templates and the fallback path, plus optionally a self-hosted open-weight VLM with batch-invariant inference for confidential or repeatable runs |
| Schemas and validation | Pydantic or JSON Schema models; per-broker YAML mapping configs in Git |
| Canonical store and matching | DuckDB or Polars over Parquet; SQL waterfall with explicit ordering |
| Review UI | Lightweight web app (e.g., Streamlit-class) showing page image with highlighted bounding box, per-field confirm, reason codes |
| Audit trail | Append-only JSONL or event table with hash chaining; optional W3C PROV/OpenLineage export |
| Evidence pack | Excel/PDF export in the firm's working-paper format plus reproducibility package |
| QA harness | Golden-set scorer, seeded-break scorer, rerun-hash comparer, drift dashboard |

### Measurable acceptance criteria

Fix these at Gate 0, together with the manual baseline method. Targets marked "proposed" are our recommendations, not published standards.

| Criterion | Definition | Proposed target | Measured at |
|---|---|---|---|
| False matches | Broker trade auto-matched to the wrong internal trade, or a break reported as agreed | **0** across golden and scale sets (supports < ~0.0015% at 95% on 200k) | Gates 2, 3 |
| Row completeness | Broker rows captured ÷ broker rows in document; reconciled to totals and counts | **100%**; every shortfall detected as COMPLETENESS_FAIL | Gates 1, 2 |
| Seeded-break recall | Seeded breaks surfaced with the correct category | **100%** detection; ≥ 98% correct primary code (proposed) | Gate 2 |
| Undetected critical-field errors | Wrong critical-field values in auto-accepted rows (not routed to review) | **0** on sealed test set; ≥ 300 sampled rows per stratum | Gates 1, 3 |
| Field accuracy (pre-review) | Exact match after normalisation, per critical field, per stratum | Report, no gate; used to size review load | Gate 1 |
| Document-level exact rate | Documents with every field correct | Report; guards against field-level optimism (0.949 vs 0.700 lesson) | Gate 1 |
| Deterministic rerun | Re-execute from frozen inputs and configuration | **Byte-identical** output hash, 10 of 10 reruns, including on different hardware | Gate 2 |
| AI-path stability | Field agreement across 3 re-extractions on the AI path | Report; 100% of disagreements routed to review | Gate 2 |
| Exception separation | Each exception carries exactly one primary code from the taxonomy | 100% | Gate 2 |
| Review rate | Share of rows routed to human review | Report per stratum; target set after Gate 1 baseline | Gates 1–3 |
| Reviewer time | Minutes per 1,000 trades and per exception vs a timed manual run on the same data | Proposed ≥ 50% reduction with equal or better seeded-break catch rate | Gates 2, 3 |
| Reviewer vigilance | Catch rate of seeded canaries in review queues | ≥ 95% (proposed) | Gate 3 |
| New-format onboarding | Elapsed effort from first sight of a held-back format to an approved, regression-passing template | Proposed ≤ 1 person-day for native PDF/Excel; report for scans | Gate 2 |
| Throughput and cost | End-to-end time and cost for the scale set | Proposed full 200k–500k-trade cycle within one working day on a single node; cost reported | Gate 2 |
| Evidence pack | Inspector-style re-performance by someone outside the build team | Passes without builder assistance | Gate 4 |

A manual baseline can be framed simply: time a reviewer reconciling a slice of the synthetic set by hand. Pre-AI confirmation data gives the flavour. One practice reported about 10–15 minutes per bank account on paper confirmations ([Confirmation.com Zinman case study](https://www.confirmation.com/media/53080/case-study_zinman-co_final.pdf)).

### Phase gates

| Gate | Purpose | Entry | Exit criteria | Indicative duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| G0 Framing and approvals | Fix scope, criteria, baseline and governance | Sponsor, methodology, IT security, independence and legal contacts named | Written success criteria; synthetic-only data policy; data-handling and model-hosting decision; intended-use statement drafted | 2–3 weeks |
| G1 Extraction | Prove complete, grounded extraction | Generator and golden set built | 100% completeness detection; 0 undetected critical-field errors in auto-accepted rows on sealed set; per-stratum metrics reported | 4–6 weeks |
| G2 End-to-end reconciliation | Prove matching, exceptions, repeatability and scale | G1 passed | 0 false matches; 100% seeded-break recall; byte-identical reruns; onboarding of 2 held-back formats timed; scale set processed; reviewer-time baseline beaten | 4–6 weeks |
| G3 Parallel run | Test on real documents alongside the manual procedure | G2 passed; client consent, secrecy and transfer assessment, engagement-letter wording, hosting approved | Same exceptions found as manual or better; reperformance sample clean; reviewer vigilance metrics met; issues logged | One cycle (e.g., a year-end or interim date) |
| G4 Methodology and quality review | Decide on certification for wider use | G3 passed | Evidence pack accepted by methodology/QA; ISQM 1 certification file (validation results, change control, monitoring KPIs); go/no-go documented | 3–4 weeks |

The PoC should explicitly *not* attempt autonomous agents in the matching path, valuation testing, or ingestion of client-supplied documents as if they were confirmations. Each is a separate decision with its own evidence implications.

## Regulatory and industry perspectives: international, US, UK and EU

### International (IAASB, IFIAR, IESBA)

Under the ISAs, **ISA 505** (clarified 2008, effective for periods beginning on or after 15 December 2009) remains in force. It treats confirmations "in paper form, or by electronic or other medium" as direct written responses to the auditor ([ISA 505 text](https://www.ifac.org/_flysystem/azure-private/publications/files/A024%202013%20IAASB%20Handbook%20ISA%20505.pdf); [IAASB ISA 505 project](https://www.iaasb.org/consultations-projects/external-confirmations-isa-505)). Its core requirements are maintaining control over requests, responding to doubts about reliability (including the integrity of electronic transmission), and investigating exceptions. These are cited from the standard text and were not re-verified this session. A revised ISA 505 sits in the separate "Targeted Standards in the ISA 500 Series" project. The IAASB discussed first drafts in September 2026 and plans to approve exposure drafts in December 2026 ([IAASB project updates](https://www.iaasb.org/iaasb-consultations/latest-our-projects)). The broader audit-evidence package, proposed ISA 500, 330 and 520 (Revised), was re-exposed in August 2026 with comments due 15 December 2026 and at least 18 months proposed for implementation ([IAASB focus area](https://www.iaasb.org/focus-areas/strengthening-risk-based-audit-framework-proposed-revisions-isa-330-isa-500-and-isa-520)). Engagements in 2026–2028 therefore rely on extant ISA 500, including para 9 on the reliability of information produced by the entity. ISQM 1 para 32(f) and (h) require appropriate technological resources, including those from service providers ([ICAEW on ISQM 1](https://www.icaew.com/technical/audit-and-assurance/Audit/quality-management-in-audit-firms/ISQM-1-Use-of-resources-obtained-from-service-providers)). If a tool is not firm-approved, the engagement partner must show it is appropriate ([AUASB standards portal](https://standards.auasb.gov.au/node/1477)). The IAASB's February 2026 roundtables called human-in-the-loop oversight "nonnegotiable". They singled out non-deterministic outputs as a practical difficulty, and the IAASB chose non-authoritative guidance over new requirements ([IAASB roundtable outcomes](https://ifacweb.blob.core.windows.net/publicfiles/2026-02/IAASB-Technology-Quality-Management-Roundtables-Feedback.pdf)). IAPN 1000 covers trade confirmations and clearing houses for financial instruments as non-authoritative practice ([IAPN 1000](https://accountant.nl/siteassets/accountant.nl/diversen/iapn_1000_dec2011.pdf)). IFIAR's April 2026 report warns about over-reliance on outputs "without sufficient challenge" ([FAOA news on IFIAR 2026](https://www.rab-asr.ch/en/news/ifiar-report-2026-use-technology-audits-innovation-and-audit-quality)). IESBA's June 2026 snapshot confirms that confidentiality applies regardless of tool and that using client data for AI training needs clear authorisation ([IESBA snapshot](https://www.ethicsboard.org/news-events/2026-06/iesba-snapshot-ethics-and-independence-approach-use-technology)).

### United States (PCAOB, AICPA, banking supervisors)

For issuer audits, the new **PCAOB AS 2310** applies to fiscal years ending on or after 15 June 2025. It is principles-based across all confirmation methods and the most prescriptive framework on intermediaries. An auditor should not use an intermediary whose controls are ineffective, or that the company could override, unless the risk can be addressed otherwise ([PCAOB confirmation resources](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/implementation-resources-PCAOB-standards-rules/confirmation); [PCAOB Release 2023-008](https://assets.pcaobus.org/pcaob-dev/docs/default-source/rulemaking/docket_028/2023-008_confirmation-adopting-release.pdf)). The **technology-assisted analysis amendments** apply to fiscal years beginning on or after 15 December 2025. AS 1105 .10A requires the auditor to understand the source of company-provided external electronic information and the company's process for it, and to test the information or the controls. A Board policy statement (September 2025) and staff examples (October 2025) allow risk-based judgement on when separate testing is unnecessary ([SEC Release 34-100774](https://sec.gov/files/rules/pcaob/2024/34-100774.pdf); [PCAOB policy statement](https://pcaobus.org/news-events/news-releases/news-release-detail/pcaob-posts-board-policy-statement-on-evaluating-the-reliability-of-external-electronic-information-provided-by-the-company)). The design consequence is direct. Broker statements obtained directly are confirmation evidence. The same statements supplied by the client fall under .10A, and the tool's channel provenance and hashing is how the auditor evidences that the information is unmodified. The PCAOB's 2024 GenAI Spotlight says users remain responsible for outputs and reviewers must apply the same diligence ([PCAOB Spotlight](https://pcaobus.org/documents/generative-ai-spotlight.pdf)). For non-issuers, the AICPA approved **SAS No. 150, External Confirmations**, on 14 May 2026, effective for periods ending on or after 15 December 2028. It addresses intermediaries and direct access to information held by external sources ([AICPA](https://www.aicpa-cima.com/news/article/aicpa-updates-standards-involving-audit-confirmations); [Deloitte DART](https://dart.deloitte.com/USDART/home/news/all-news/2026/jul/aicpa-standard-on-external-confirmations)). In banking supervision, **SR 11-7 no longer exists**. On 17 April 2026 the Fed, OCC and FDIC replaced it with SR 26-2, which is principles-based, aimed mainly at banks with assets over $30bn, and described by Vice Chair Bowman as applying "narrowly to traditional models and basic AI applications". The effect is to leave generative and agentic AI outside its scope ([Federal Reserve SR 26-2](https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/SR2602.htm); [Bowman speech](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/bowman20260501a.htm)). The practical gap is being filled by the NIST AI RMF and its GenAI profile, NIST AI 600-1, which names "confabulation" as a risk ([NIST AI 600-1](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf)), and by Treasury's Financial Services AI RMF (February 2026, about 230 control objectives) ([American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/treasury-issues-new-ai-risk-tools-for-banks)). None of these binds an auditor of a commodities trader, but they are the vocabulary a technology team will meet.

### United Kingdom (FRC, PRA)

The UK moved ahead of the IAASB on confirmations. The FRC's revised **ISA (UK) 505** applies to periods commencing on or after 15 December 2024 ([ICAEW](https://www.icaew.com/technical/audit-and-assurance/faculty-resources/audit-and-beyond/2024/articles/updating-and-enhancing-external-audit-confirmations)). The FRC's June 2025 guidance on AI in audit is non-prescriptive. It asks for central and on-file documentation of a tool's function, development, governance, training material and output explainability. It was paired with a thematic review that frames certification as a lifecycle from needs analysis to monitoring ([FRC news release](https://www.frc.org.uk/news-and-events/news/2025/06/frc-publishes-landmark-guidance-providing-clarity-to-audit-profession-on-the-uses-of-ai/)). The PRA's SS1/23 keeps AI and ML inside its general model definition rather than carving GenAI out ([Covington Global Policy Watch](https://www.globalpolicywatch.com/2026/04/uk-financial-services-regulators-approach-to-artificial-intelligence-in-2026/)). The Bank of England has flagged concentration risk in third-party model providers ([BoE response to TSC](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/-/media/boe/files/letter/2026/response-to-tsc-inquiry-report-on-ai-in-financial-services)), which argues for a provider exit plan.

### European Union (AI Act, GDPR)

The AI Act was amended by the AI Omnibus, **Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force 27 July 2026**. It defers Annex III high-risk obligations to 2 December 2027 and softens Article 4 AI literacy to "take measures to support". GPAI obligations were not delayed ([White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/eu-ai-omnibus-enters-force-amending-ai-act); [Jones Walker](https://www.joneswalker.com/en/insights/blogs/ai-law-blog/yes-august-2-still-matters-the-eu-approved-a-high-risk-ai-delay-but-most-trans.html)). Statutory audit is not an Annex III category, and a data-capture and reconciliation aid fits the "narrow procedural task" logic of Art. 6(3). The tool is therefore very unlikely to be high-risk. The firm's obligations reduce mainly to deployer-level AI literacy and governance, plus reliance on its GPAI provider's compliance. This classification is our inference and should be confirmed by counsel. GDPR applies to any natural-person data in broker documents.

### Industry practice (FIA, exchanges, ISDA, audit networks)

Market infrastructure defines the matching vocabulary. FIA's DMIST has standardised give-up and allocation timeliness, with 30-minute steps ([Mondo Visione](https://mondovisione.com/media-and-resources/news/fia-dmist-publishes-trade-allocations-standard-and-launches-consultation-on-av-2023615/)). Its average-pricing standard requires minimum CCP functionality, set out in a 16-point table ([A-Team Insight](https://a-teaminsight.com/?p=68738)). CME FIXML carries AllocInd, AvgPxInd and the allocation group ID GrpID2, which are the natural group keys for T3 matching ([CME STP FIXML TradeCaptureReport](https://cmegroupclientsite.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EPICSANDBOX/pages/599196174/CME+STP+FIXML+-+TradeCaptureReport+-+Futures+and+Options)). LME give-ups depend on an agreed customer code in a public reference field ([LME Rules on Give-Ups](https://www.lme.com/-/media/Files/Company/Market-regulation/Key-compliance-notices/Rules-Powers-and-Functions/00-383-A376-R010-Rules-on-LME-Give-Ups.pdf)). ISDA names confirmation processing as a GenAI use case ([ISDA, April 2024](https://www.isda.org/a/PbwgE/GenAI-in-the-Derivatives-Market-A-Future-Perspective.pdf)). Among audit networks, all the large firms have embedded GenAI or agents in their platforms. Examples are KPMG Clara agents for more than 95,000 auditors in April 2025 ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/xx/en/media/press-releases/2025/04/kpmg-advances-ai-integration-in-kpmg-clara-smart-audit-platform.html)) and EY's multi-agent framework across about 130,000 assurance professionals in April 2026 ([EY](https://www.ey.com/en_us/newsroom/2026/04/ey-launches-enterprise-scale-agentic-ai-to-redefine-the-audit-experience-for-the-ai-era)). Public messaging stresses vouching, tie-outs and human-in-the-loop. No network announcement specific to AI processing of confirmations was found. One cloud provider's derivative-confirms reference design is instructive: new counterparty schemas are mapped by humans through a review interface rather than inferred by the model ([AWS derivative confirms](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/automate-derivative-confirms-processing-using-aws-ai-services-for-the-capital-markets-industry/)).

## Swiss-specific considerations

### Swiss auditing standards and oversight

Swiss statutory audits follow the **Swiss Standards on Auditing (SA-CH)** published by EXPERTsuisse. They are based on the ISAs (as ISA-CH) and ISQM-CH. A 2026 edition contains the revised ISA-CH and ISQM-CH and an updated framework aligned with the current international version ([EXPERTsuisse SA-CH](https://expertsuisse.ch/de/produkte/pub-2-sach/die-schweizer-standards-zur-abschlusspruefung-sa-ch)). Swiss practice tracks the IAASB, so **no revised ISA-CH 500 or 505 exists**. The extant ISA-CH 500, 505, 540, 230 and 220 and ISQM-CH 1 govern a 2026–2028 engagement. A revised ISA-CH 505 can follow only after IAASB final approval, likely 2027 or later, plus an implementation period. We could not confirm the effective date of the SA-CH 2026 edition or which standards it changes. The FAOA's Rundschreiben 1/2009 (status 15 December 2025) governs which standards are recognised for audits under its oversight ([FAOA Rundschreiben 1/2009](https://www.rab-asr.ch/sites/default/files/2026-02/Rundschreiben_1_2009__Stand_am_15__Dezember_2025_.pdf)). If the engagement involves a component of a US SEC registrant, AS 2310 and AS 1105 .10A apply as well. The FAOA's 2025 annual report records that the five largest audit firms increased their automated tools and techniques over three years. It also reports 15 inspections covering 42 engagements, and average findings per file rising from 0.6 to 0.8 ([FAOA Annual Report 2025](https://www.rab-asr.ch/sites/default/files/2026-04/Annual%20report%202025.pdf)). The FAOA's earlier stance is supportive of technology ([FAOA Annual Report 2023](https://www.rab-asr.ch/sites/default/files/2025-10/Annual_Report_2023.pdf)), and it publicises IFIAR's technology work. We found no FAOA statement on certifying AI tools comparable to the FRC thematic review. EXPERTsuisse offers a members-only white paper on AI and a guide on ChatGPT in auditing ([EXPERTsuisse KI & Digitalisierung](https://expertsuisse.ch/de/publikationen-fachsupport/fachthemen/ki-und-digitalisierung)). The Swiss Federal Audit Office has published a guide to auditing AI systems ([EFK Leitfaden Audit KI-Systeme](https://www.efk.admin.ch/wp-content/uploads/publikationen/vorgehen-und-methoden/leitfaden-audit-ki-systems-de.pdf)). We did not review the contents of either.

### Auditor secrecy is the sharpest constraint

For a Swiss auditor, client confidentiality outranks data protection as a design driver. **Art. 321 StGB** expressly covers auditors bound to secrecy under the Code of Obligations, together with their auxiliary persons ([k-i-soft checklist](https://www.k-i-soft.ch/blog/berufsgeheimnis-und-cloud/); [StGB on Fedlex](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/54/757_781_799/de)). Whether a cloud or LLM provider can be a permissible auxiliary is legally disputed. A strict view, from an opinion for the Zurich data protection commissioner, holds that disclosure without specific consent is permissible only if indispensable and foreseeable ([datenrecht.ch on Wohlers](https://datenrecht.ch/en/wohlers-auslagerung-einer-datenbearbeitung-und-das-berufsgeheimnis-art-321-stgb/)). A more permissive view treats encryption with no provider-held key as "no disclosure" ([Rosenthal](https://www.rosenthal.ch/downloads/Rosenthal-CloudLawfulAccess.pdf)). Providers subject to the US CLOUD Act add uncertainty that contracts cannot fully remove ([gawel.ch](https://gawel.ch/insights/17-cloud-contracts-jurisdiction/)). Art. 730b(2) CO, which requires statutory auditors to keep findings and business secrets confidential, adds to this. That reference was not re-verified this session ([Code of Obligations on Fedlex](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/27/317_321_377/de)). The architecture above suits this constraint well. The deterministic template path sends nothing to any model at run time. AI is needed mainly for onboarding, where layout samples can be **pseudonymised or replaced with synthetic values**, and for the scan fallback. The resulting hosting order of preference is a self-hosted open-weight model on own or Swiss infrastructure, which is also best for repeatability; then a Swiss- or EU-region private deployment with customer-managed keys, no-training and no-retention terms and named sub-processors; and last a US-headquartered API, which needs client consent in the engagement letter and pseudonymisation of identifiers.

### nFADP and cross-border transfers

The revised **Federal Act on Data Protection** (in force 1 September 2023) is technology-neutral. The FDPIC states it applies directly to AI-supported processing ([FDPIC on AI](https://www.edoeb.admin.ch/en/ai-and-data-protection)). Broker documents for a corporate trader carry mostly corporate rather than personal data, but trader names, contact persons and signatories bring the FADP into play. Cross-border disclosure requires either a Federal Council adequacy listing or Art. 16(2) safeguards such as recognised SCCs with a transfer assessment. For the US, adequacy covers **only Swiss-US DPF-certified recipients, effective 15 September 2024** ([Lenz & Staehelin](https://www.lenzstaehelin.com/news-and-insights/browse-thought-leadership-insights/insights-detail/swiss-us-data-privacy-framework-swiss-adequacy-decision-for-us-certified-data-recipients/)). The DPF rests on a US Executive Order, which is a noted durability risk. Further FADP points come from prior knowledge and were not re-verified: processor contracts and sub-processor authorisation (Art. 9), privacy by design (Art. 7), a DPIA for high-risk processing (Art. 22), and criminal fines of up to CHF 250,000 on responsible individuals for certain intentional breaches ([FADP on Fedlex](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/en)). We found no FDPIC guidance specific to transfers to LLM providers.

### Swiss AI regulation and EU AI Act reach

Switzerland has **no horizontal AI law**. On 12 February 2025 the Federal Council chose a sector-specific approach, with ratification of the Council of Europe AI Convention (signed 27 March 2025) implemented through targeted amendments. A consultation draft was mandated by end-2026 ([White & Case AI Watch – Switzerland](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-our-thinking/ai-watch-global-regulatory-tracker-switzerland); [BAKOM](https://www.bakom.admin.ch/en/artificial-intelligence)). Secondary sources suggest the draft may slip to early 2027, with entry into force 2028 at the earliest ([Chambers AI 2026 – Switzerland](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/artificial-intelligence-2026/switzerland/trends-and-developments)). Status as of October 2026 is unconfirmed against admin.ch. **FINMA Guidance 08/2024** (December 2024) is the best domestic template for AI governance expectations: an inventory with risk classification, clear responsibilities, testing and monitoring, documentation, explainability and independent review ([Lenz & Staehelin on FINMA 08/2024](https://www.lenzstaehelin.com/news-and-insights/browse-thought-leadership-insights/insights-detail/finma-issues-guidance-on-ai-use-in-financial-institutions/)). It binds FINMA-supervised institutions, not audit firms. A commodities trading company is typically not FINMA-supervised, which is our inference and depends on the group's activities, so the guidance serves as a benchmark rather than a requirement. The **EU AI Act** reaches Swiss firms when they place systems on the EU market or when an AI system's output is used in the Union under Art. 2(1)(c). Commentators diverge on how broad that trigger is ([EC AI Act Service Desk, Art. 2](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-2); [William Fry](https://www.williamfry.com/knowledge/a-practical-guide-to-the-extraterritorial-reach-of-the-ai-act/)). Reconciliation output used in audits of EU group entities could make the firm a deployer in scope. Because the use case is not high-risk, the practical consequence is AI-literacy measures and governance hygiene rather than conformity assessment.

## Premise corrections and evidence caveats

Five premises in common circulation are wrong or out of date, and the report reflects the corrected position. First, **ISA 500 (Revised) was not finalised in 2024 or 2025**. The 2022 exposure draft was never finalised, and a combined ISA 500/330/520 package was re-exposed in August 2026 with comments due 15 December 2026 ([IAASB publication page](https://www.iaasb.org/publications/proposed-revisions-audit-evidence-risk-response-isa-330-isa-500-isa-520)). Second, **ISA 505 is revised in a separate project**, with exposure drafts planned for December 2026, and is excluded from the ISA 500 package's conforming amendments ([IAASB Overall Project ED](https://ifacweb.blob.core.windows.net/publicfiles/2026-08/IAASB-Audit-Evidence-Risk-Response-Overall-Project-Exposure-Draft.pdf)). Third, **SR 11-7 was rescinded on 17 April 2026** and replaced by SR 26-2 (with OCC and FDIC equivalents). The new guidance excludes GenAI and agentic AI, and the OCC bulletin numbering (2026-13 versus a 2026-14 entry) is inconsistent in the OCC's own index ([OCC 2026 bulletins](https://www.occ.gov/news-events/newsroom/news-issuances-by-year/bulletins/2026-bulletins.html)). Fourth, **EU AI Act Annex III high-risk obligations no longer start on 2 August 2026**. They were deferred to 2 December 2027 by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, although some earlier sources still show the old date. Fifth, the AICPA's confirmations update is now final as **SAS No. 150, effective for periods ending on or after 15 December 2028**, a year later than its exposure draft proposed. We did not verify whether its final wording on confirmation platforms matches the draft.

The evidence base has limits that the reader should weigh. The research environment had restricted web access. Many findings, especially arXiv results, PCAOB, FRC and IAASB texts, and vendor pages, come from search-engine summaries rather than full-text reads. Standard paragraph references such as ISA 505 paras 7–14 and ISA 500 para 9 rest on the standard text as previously known and were not re-verified. Benchmark figures (GriTS, TEDS, AUC, hallucination-free rates) are as reported in abstracts or secondary summaries. Several come from vendor-run benchmarks: PP-OCRv6, ParseBench, RD-TableBench. Determinism overhead figures (34% versus 61.5%) are secondhand. Engine benchmarks are single-author. Vendor STP and accuracy claims are unaudited. No published benchmark exists for derivatives broker statements, and no independent case study exists of AI reconciling broker confirmations to a client trade list in either operations or audit. No public break-rate statistic for listed-derivatives broker reconciliation was found. For these reasons the PoC acceptance targets above are proposals derived from audit logic, not industry standards, and the PoC itself is the instrument that turns them into measured facts.

## Conclusion

The central insight is that "AI reconciliation" is the wrong unit of design for audit evidence. The right unit is an **evidence pipeline in which determinism is the default and AI is an accountable contributor at specific, verifiable points**. Framed that way, the questions regulators worry about lose most of their force. Non-deterministic outputs, explainability, fitness for purpose and automation bias do not disappear, but each has a mechanical answer. The evidential object is a frozen, hashed extraction re-performable without the model. Every value is grounded to a document coordinate. Every status traces to a named rule. Every human judgement is recorded as a disposition layered over the machine result. The same framing explains why the binding constraints are data and governance. Once the architecture removes the model from the decision, what remains is knowing what each broker's codes, units and groupings mean, proving where each document came from, and persuading methodology, quality and legal functions that the evidence pack stands up. For a Swiss auditor, secrecy under Art. 321 StGB is the most binding of these.

Two implications follow for the engagement and technology teams. First, the most valuable asset the PoC creates is not the extractor but the **synthetic, seeded, sealed golden dataset and its regression harness**. That asset outlives any model snapshot, lets every model or template change be certified quantitatively, and supplies the monitoring KPIs the FRC found missing at large firms. Second, the economics invert expectations. Compute and model fees are trivial at this scale, and reviewer attention is the scarce resource. Investment should therefore go first into completeness controls and a review interface that forces verification, rather than into chasing the next point of extraction accuracy from a newer model.
