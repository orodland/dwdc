# Audit Standards, Regulator Positions and Audit-Profession Perspectives on AI in External Confirmation and Reconciliation Procedures for Derivatives (as of October 2026)

Scope note: generic scenario only. An auditor uses an AI-enabled tool to ingest broker or counterparty confirmations (PDF, email, SWIFT/portal or platform feeds), extract trade terms, reconcile them to the entity's derivative positions or trade listings, and flag exceptions. No client or engagement is identified.

Research-method caveat: during this session the egress proxy blocked direct page fetches of pcaobus.org, frc.org.uk, iaasb/ifacweb, fsa.go.jp and rab-asr.ch, so most findings come from search-result extracts of those primary pages plus secondary summaries (Deloitte DART, law firms, professional bodies). Where a paragraph number or detail comes from the standard text as I already knew it, rather than from a source retrieved in this session, it is marked "[standard text, not re-verified this session]". The report writer should treat those as needing a final check against the official text.

**Important correction to the brief's premise:** ISA 500 (Revised) was NOT approved as a final standard in 2024/2025. The 2022 exposure draft was never finalised. The IAASB re-exposed a combined package (proposed ISA 500 (Revised), ISA 330 (Revised), ISA 520 (Revised)) in August 2026, with comments due 15 December 2026. ISA 505 and ISA 501 are in a separate "Targeted Standards in the ISA 500 Series" project, with an exposure draft planned for approval in December 2026. AS 2310 (PCAOB) has been in force since FY ending on or after 15 June 2025. The AICPA's new confirmations standard (SAS No. 150) was issued in 2026, effective for periods ending on or after 15 December 2028. Details are below.

## 1. External confirmation standards: ISA 505, PCAOB AS 2310, AICPA AU-C 505 (SAS 150). Control, reliability, electronic confirmations, exceptions, and use of tools to process responses

### Takeaway
All three frameworks require the auditor to keep control over the confirmation process from request to receipt, to judge the reliability of responses (especially electronic ones and those routed through intermediaries), and to investigate exceptions and non-responses. None of them distinguishes between people and tools when processing responses. An AI tool that reads or reconciles confirmations therefore has to sit inside the auditor-controlled chain, keep the original response and its provenance, and leave every exception and every judgement about reliability to the auditor. PCAOB AS 2310 is the most prescriptive of the three on intermediaries and is already in force. SAS 150 (US non-issuers) follows in 2028, and a revised ISA 505 is expected to be exposed for comment around December 2026 to early 2027.

### Cited Findings

**PCAOB AS 2310, The Auditor's Use of Confirmation (US issuer audits)**
- The new AS 2310 replaced the old AS 2310, *The Confirmation Process*, in its entirety. The PCAOB says it applies to audits of fiscal years ending on or after 15 June 2025. — [PCAOB implementation resources: Confirmation](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/implementation-resources-PCAOB-standards-rules/confirmation); [IAS Plus summary](https://www.iasplus.com/en-ca/standards/assurance/other-standards/pcaob-as-2310-the-auditor2019s-use-of-confirmation-and-other-amendments-to-pcaob-standard)
- The Board's stated reasons include the growing use of electronic communications and third-party intermediaries in confirmations. The new requirements are "more principles-based" and designed to apply to all methods of confirmation, including electronic communications and intermediaries. — [PCAOB implementation resources](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/implementation-resources-pcaob-standards-rules/confirmation); [PCAOB Release No. 2023-008 adopting release](https://assets.pcaobus.org/pcaob-dev/docs/default-source/rulemaking/docket_028/2023-008_confirmation-adopting-release.pdf)
- The auditor must maintain control over confirmation requests and responses. "Maintaining control" means establishing direct communication between the intended confirming party and the auditor, so that responses are reliable. — [AS 2310 standard page](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS2310); [PCAOB Spotlight: Observations and reminders on the confirmation process](https://pcaobus.org/documents/observations-reminders-confirmation-process-spotlight.pdf)
- Intermediaries: when a service provider facilitates electronic confirmation, the auditor must still keep control. Some auditors have used SOC reports to assess an intermediary's controls. Under the new standard, the auditor should not use an intermediary if its controls are not operating effectively and the auditor cannot address the risk of interception or alteration through other procedures, or if circumstances allow the company to override those controls. — [PCAOB Release No. 2023-008](https://assets.pcaobus.org/pcaob-dev/docs/default-source/rulemaking/docket_028/2023-008_confirmation-adopting-release.pdf); [SEC notice of filing 34-98689](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/pcaob/2023/34-98689.pdf)
- Negative confirmation requests alone do not provide sufficient appropriate audit evidence under the new standard. — [Schneider Downs summary of new AS 2310](https://schneiderdowns.com/our-thoughts-on/pcaobs-new-standard/)
- Alternative procedures are required where the auditor cannot identify a confirming party, a response is unreliable, there is a non-response or incomplete response, or an intermediary cannot be used. — [PCAOB Release No. 2023-008](https://assets.pcaobus.org/pcaob-dev/docs/default-source/rulemaking/docket_028/2023-008_confirmation-adopting-release.pdf)
- The PCAOB published a side-by-side comparison of AS 2310 with ISA 505 and AU-C 505. — [PCAOB comparison document](https://assets.pcaobus.org/pcaob-dev/docs/default-source/rulemaking/docket_028/comparison-as-2310-with-isa-505-and-au-c-section-505.pdf?sfvrsn=b1f1b5d3_4)

**Practice and tooling context**
- Confirmation platforms route requests to banks and counterparties and return the responses. Practitioner sources stress that the auditor still reviews responses and handles exceptions. — [Circit: How technology can help with ISA 505](https://www.circit.io/post/isa-505-external-confirmations); [DataSnipper guide for the AI-curious auditor](https://www.datasnipper.com/resources/the-ultimate-guide-for-the-ai-curious-auditor) (vendor sources; treat as practice description, not authority)

### Inferences
- An AI reconciliation tool that ingests broker confirmations is not itself the "intermediary" in the AS 2310 sense if responses reach the auditor directly and the tool runs inside the auditor's environment. If the tool or its vendor sits in the transmission path (for example, it receives broker emails or portal feeds), it starts to look like an intermediary. The auditor would then need evidence on its controls over interception and alteration (for example, a SOC 1/SOC 2 or ISAE 3402/3000 report plus complementary user controls), and on whether the client could override it.
- To keep "control" and reliability, the tool should: (a) keep the original response file or message with metadata (sender domain, headers, timestamps, hash), (b) record lineage from each extracted field back to its location in the source document, (c) never let entity personnel upload or edit responses, and (d) flag responses that arrive through unexpected channels or senders.
- Reconciling extracted broker terms (notional, trade date, maturity, strike/rate, counterparty, side, fair value, collateral or margin) to the entity's records is the "compare response to records" step. Every mismatch the tool flags is an "exception" the auditor must investigate under ISA 505 para 14 / AS 2310. The tool can sort exceptions (for example, timing versus substantive) but cannot close them on its own authority.
- Because the AICPA and the IAASB will finalise new confirmation standards between 2027 and 2028, a tool designed now should meet the strictest current bar (AS 2310's intermediary rules) to stay usable across frameworks.

## 2. Audit evidence and technology: ISA 500 (and the 2026 re-exposure), PCAOB AS 1105/AS 2301 technology-assisted analysis amendments, IPE, ISA 220 / ISQM 1, ISA 230, ISA 540 for derivatives

### Takeaway
Under the PCAOB, the technology-assisted analysis amendments (AS 1105 .10A and the related AS 2301 changes) took effect for fiscal years beginning on or after 15 December 2025. They require the auditor to understand the source of external information that the company supplies in electronic form and the company's process for it, and then to test that information or the relevant controls. Internationally, ISA 500 (Revised) is still at exposure-draft stage. The August 2026 ED package (ISA 500/330/520) strengthens the requirements on judging relevance and reliability and on professional skepticism, and proposes at least 18 months for implementation after final approval, so it cannot apply before roughly 2029. ISQM 1 32(f) and ISA 220 (Revised) already require appropriate technological resources, and ISA 230 requires documentation that an experienced auditor could understand.

**PCAOB technology-assisted analysis amendments (AS 1105, AS 2301)**

### Inferences
- For a broker-confirmation reconciliation tool under PCAOB standards: if the auditor gets confirmations directly (AS 2310), they are external evidence obtained by the auditor. If the auditor instead relies on broker statements or trade files supplied by the company, AS 1105 .10A applies. The auditor must understand the source and the company's process, and must test for modification or test controls. The tool's provenance features (hashes, sender authentication, comparison to a broker portal accessed directly) are a natural way to meet .10A(b).
- AI extraction adds its own reliability layer. Even when the source document is reliable, the extracted data is only as reliable as the extraction. Under ISA 500 and AS 1105 the auditor needs evidence that the extraction is accurate and complete, for example through firm-level validation of the tool plus engagement-level checks such as sample reperformance against source and control totals (record counts, notional totals by counterparty).
- A confirmation proves existence, rights and obligations, and terms. It does not on its own prove valuation of derivatives. Broker or counterparty "marks" on confirmations are evidence that must be evaluated under ISA 540 / AS 2501 (independent price verification, model testing). The tool should keep "terms reconciliation" separate from "valuation comparison".
- Context from prior knowledge, not re-verified this session: PCAOB AS 2501 (Revised), *Auditing Accounting Estimates, Including Fair Value Measurements*, which superseded AS 2502/2503 for FYs ending on or after 15 December 2020, is the relevant PCAOB standard for derivative valuation.
- The proposed ISA 500 is unlikely to be effective before roughly 2029 (approval no earlier than 2027 plus at least 18 months). Engagements in 2026 to 2028 under ISAs therefore rely on extant ISA 500, ISA 505, ISQM 1 and ISA 220.

## 3. Regulator and standard-setter statements on AI in audit: PCAOB, FRC, IAASB, IFIAR, ICAEW/AICPA/CPA bodies, EU AI Act

### Takeaway
Regulators agree that existing standards apply in full, AI does not dilute responsibility, and human supervision, review and documentation are mandatory. The PCAOB (2024 Spotlight), the FRC (June 2025 guidance plus a thematic review of tool certification), the IAASB (2024 Technology Position, February 2026 roundtable feedback) and IFIAR (2025 and 2026 technology reports) have all published on the topic. None has issued an AI-specific auditing standard. The IAASB has chosen non-authoritative guidance. Inspection data shows a growing number of findings where tools were not used as intended.

### Cited Findings

**PCAOB**
- On 22 July 2024 the staff issued a Spotlight, "Staff Update on Outreach Activities Related to the Integration of Generative AI in Audits and Financial Reporting". It is based on outreach to US global network firms and several non-affiliated firms auditing more than 100 issuers, which together audit most issuer market cap. — [PCAOB Spotlight PDF](https://pcaobus.org/documents/generative-ai-spotlight.pdf); [PCAOB news release](https://pcaobus.org/news-events/news-releases/news-release-detail/pcaob-staff-shares-observations-from-outreach-on-use-of-generative-artificial-intelligence-in-audits-and-financial-reporting)
- Findings: GenAI use in audits was mainly administrative and research, although most firms saw potential in planning and performing audits. ## 4. Tool validation and certification in audit firms (ISQM 1), documentation, audit trail and reperformability

### Takeaway
Firms are expected to certify automated tools centrally before release, across a lifecycle of needs analysis, design and development, certification for implementation, and maintenance and monitoring. Engagement teams are then expected to document how the tool was used and how its outputs were evaluated. The FRC's 2025 thematic review found mature certification processes but weak monitoring of how tools affect audit quality: all but one of the six firms had no KPIs for these tools. If a tool has not been approved by the firm, the engagement partner carries the burden of showing it is appropriate.

### Cited Findings
- The FRC thematic review (June 2025) covered BDO, Deloitte, EY, Forvis Mazars, KPMG and PwC, and their processes to certify ATTs used for risk assessment and for obtaining audit evidence. It frames certification as a lifecycle: initial planning and needs analysis, design and development, certification for implementation, and subsequent maintenance and monitoring. — [FRC Thematic Review: Certification of Automated Tools and Techniques (June 2025)](https://mail.caalley.com/intl_cnt/frc/FRC_Thematic_Review_on_the_Certification_of_Automated_Tools_and_Techniques.pdf); [FRC news](https://www.frc.org.uk/news-and-events/news/2025/06/frc-publishes-landmark-guidance-providing-clarity-to-audit-profession-on-the-uses-of-ai/)
- Most firms had well-established certification processes that were being updated for AI-specific risks, and were building the capability to monitor ATT use and its effect on audit quality. The FRC also identified shortcomings across the development lifecycle. Press reports say the firms did not formally track how AI affects audit quality and that all but one had no KPIs for these tools. — [FRC Thematic Review](https://mail.caalley.com/intl_cnt/frc/FRC_Thematic_Review_on_the_Certification_of_Automated_Tools_and_Techniques.pdf); [Scottish Financial News](https://www.scottishfinancialnews.com/articles/frc-major-accounting-firms-using-ai-without-quality-oversight)
- FRC documentation principles: document centrally (function, development, governance, training material, explainability) and on the audit file. They are linked to ISQM (UK) 1 and ISA (UK) 230. — [FRC news](https://www.frc.org.uk/news-and-events/news/2025/06/frc-publishes-landmark-guidance-providing-clarity-to-audit-profession-on-the-uses-of-ai/); [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=33d716e3-44e9-432c-b707-8655df9af12f)
- ISQM 1 32(f)/(h): the firm must obtain or develop, implement, maintain and use appropriate technological resources, including those from service providers such as audit software and analytics. — [ICAEW ISQM 1 service providers guide](https://www.icaew.com/technical/audit-and-assurance/Audit/quality-management-in-audit-firms/ISQM-1-Use-of-resources-obtained-from-service-providers); [ICAEW: Evaluating resources from service providers](https://www.icaew.com/technical/audit-and-assurance/audit/quality-management-in-audit-firms/isqm-1-and-evaluating-resources-from-service-providers)
- For a technological resource the firm has not approved, the engagement partner must obtain sufficient appropriate evidence that it is appropriate in the circumstances. — [AUASB standards portal](https://standards.auasb.gov.au/node/1477)
- IAASB roundtables (Feb 2026): practical difficulties include judging fitness for purpose for adaptive or non-deterministic tools, ongoing monitoring, documentation, and review. — [IAASB roundtable outcomes](https://ifacweb.blob.core.windows.net/publicfiles/2026-02/IAASB-Technology-Quality-Management-Roundtables-Feedback.pdf)
- IFIAR inspection data: 31% of members reported findings on tools "not used as intended" (2024 survey). — [IFIAR 2025 technology report](https://www.ifiar.org/latest-news/ifiar-releases-2025-report-on-the-use-of-technology-in-audits/)
- IAASB 2020 support material on documentation when using ATT, including AI. — [IAASB](https://www.iaasb.org/publications/non-authoritative-support-material-audit-documentation-when-using-automated-tools-and-techniques)
- Secondary: inspection deficiencies where AI anomaly tools lacked documentation of calibration, input data and evaluation of false positives. — [CalCPA](https://www.calcpa.org/whats-happening/articles/ai-standard-setting-regulation-in-audit-part-1)

### Inferences
Minimum validation and documentation package for an AI broker-confirmation reconciliation tool, drawn from the sources above:
- **Firm level (ISQM 1):** an intended-use statement (which assertions, which instruments, which document types); a model and version register; a validation dataset of real-world confirmation formats from many brokers and counterparties, with measured field-level extraction accuracy, recall on mismatches, and false-negative rates; change management and re-certification on model or prompt updates; vendor due diligence (SOC reports, data residency, no training on client data); and monitoring KPIs, which the FRC found largely missing.
- **Engagement level (ISA 220/230, AS 1215):** tool name and version; input population with completeness controls (count and notional reconciled to the entity's derivative subledger); source-document retention with hashes; field-to-source traceability; exception list with each disposition and reviewer sign-off; the human reperformance sample and its results; and the auditor's conclusion on reliability.
- **Reperformability:** generative or non-deterministic components complicate "same input, same output". Firms should fix the model version and parameters, log prompts and outputs, or use deterministic post-processing, so that an inspector can reperform or at least trace the result. This is directly relevant to the IAASB roundtable's "non-deterministic outputs" concern.

### Gaps
- No public detail was found on the specific acceptance thresholds (for example, extraction accuracy percentages) that firms use when certifying tools.
- The full text of the FRC thematic review was not read directly; findings rely on the PDF mirror's search extract and on press coverage.

## 6. Professional skepticism, automation bias and human review expectations

### Takeaway
Regulators uniformly say AI cannot replace due care and skepticism, that users remain responsible for outputs, and that reviewers must challenge AI work as they would any other work. Empirical research confirms that auditors are prone to automation bias: they substitute automated cues for careful information-seeking. AI literacy, explainability, accountability and review culture moderate the effect. Tool and process design should counter automation bias explicitly.

### Cited Findings
- The PCAOB chair said due professional care and skepticism are essential and cannot be replaced by AI. Staff said users of GenAI tools remain responsible, and reviewers must apply the same diligence. — [PCAOB news release, Jul 2024](https://pcaobus.org/news-events/news-releases/news-release-detail/pcaob-staff-shares-observations-from-outreach-on-use-of-generative-artificial-intelligence-in-audits-and-financial-reporting); [Audit Update](https://www.auditupdate.com/post/pcaob-shines-a-spotlight-on-generative-ai-in-auditing-and-financial-reporting)
- The IAASB roundtables described human-in-the-loop oversight as "nonnegotiable". The ISA 500/330/520 EDs reinforce professional skepticism throughout. — [IAASB news, Feb 2026](https://www.iaasb.org/news-events/2026-02/iaasb-publishes-global-roundtable-feedback-technology-and-quality-management); [IAASB focus area](https://www.iaasb.org/focus-areas/strengthening-risk-based-audit-framework-proposed-revisions-isa-330-isa-500-and-isa-520)
- IFIAR's 2026 report flags the risk of "over-reliance on outputs without sufficient challenge of the work performed". The 2025 report calls for training so auditors can critically interpret AI outputs. — [FAOA news on IFIAR 2026 report](https://www.rab-asr.ch/en/news/ifiar-report-2026-use-technology-audits-innovation-and-audit-quality); [Parker Russell](https://parkerrussellinternational.com/ifiar-issues-2025-report-on-audit-technology/)
- Experimental evidence: in a study of 119 professional auditors, auditors showed automation bias, using automated cues in place of careful information-seeking. — [Foundation for Auditing Research: Auditor automation usage and professional skepticism](https://foundationforauditingresearch.org/publications/auditor-automation-usage-and-professional-skepticism/)
- A systematic literature review (2021–2026, PRISMA) finds that high trust in AI, limited understanding of algorithms and efficiency pressure drive automation bias, with professional skepticism as the main control. — [ResearchGate: Automation Bias and Professional Skepticism in AI-Assisted Auditing](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/404201880_Automation_Bias_and_Professional_Skepticism_in_AI-Assisted_Auditing_A_Systematic_Literature_Review)
- A conceptual paper argues AI sharpens or erodes skepticism depending on explainability, auditor AI literacy, task structure, accountability and review culture. The best outcome is calibrated reliance. — [Riley, SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6912798) (working paper)
- A field survey of 191 auditors in Egypt found AI's positive effect on skepticism was strongest among auditors with high trait skepticism. — [ResearchGate field study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/397625309_The_Effect_of_Using_Artificial_Intelligence_Tools_on_the_Professional_Skepticism_Traits_of_Auditors_A_Field_Study)

### Inferences
Design controls against automation bias in a confirmation reconciliation tool:
- Show source-document snippets next to extracted values, rather than only "match" or "no match".
- Show confidence scores and low-confidence flags.
- Require a positive reviewer action on every exception and on a random sample of "matches".
- Include periodic seeded or known-error tests.
- Escalate responses with anomalies in sender, format or channel as possible fraud indicators (ISA 240 / AS 2401), not as data-quality noise.
- Avoid auto-clearing tolerance thresholds without auditor-set and documented parameters.

### Gaps
- No regulator text was found that sets quantitative human-review sample sizes for AI-processed confirmations.

### Inferences
- For a Swiss auditor in 2026 to 2028, the binding requirements are the extant ISA-CH 500, ISA-CH 505, ISA-CH 540, ISA-CH 230, ISA-CH 220 and ISQM-CH 1, which mirror the ISA paragraphs in sections 1, 2 and 4. Revised ISA-CH 500 and 505 can only follow IAASB final approval: likely 2027 or later, effective about 18 months after.
- If the Swiss firm audits a group component for a US SEC registrant, or reports under PCAOB standards, AS 2310 and AS 1105 .10A also apply. If it serves EU entities or uses EU-based AI vendors, EU AI Act deployer duties (for example, AI literacy) may apply extraterritorially through the vendor relationship. That needs legal confirmation.
- Swiss data protection (revDSG/nFADP, in force since September 2023 [prior knowledge, not verified this session]) and professional secrecy are relevant to cloud-hosted AI processing of confirmations. Vendor due diligence under ISQM-CH 1 32(h) should cover data location and access.
